Project goal

Desinformation erkennen. Demokratie schützen. SOURCE develops a freely accessible early-warning platform against disinformation: web content is systematically found, analysed and prepared for countermeasures, so that manipulative information campaigns are recognised early and researchers, journalists, civil society and institutions are supported in fact-checking.

At its core is an openly accessible database of potential disinformation artefacts. The project extends the Open Web Index and the OpenWebSearch.eu ecosystem with collaborative infrastructure for large-scale web and social-media analysis, provenance tracing, and practical analyst-facing workflows.

Why this project exists

Current disinformation analysis depends heavily on fragmented datasets, closed platform interfaces, or commercial search engines. SOURCE aims to reduce that dependency by providing an open European infrastructure for collecting, indexing, and analysing campaign traces across the web and social media.

The proposal combines five elements:

  • large-scale data acquisition
  • provenance-based analysis
  • content-based detection
  • community validation and dissemination
  • HPC-ready infrastructure and open interfaces

Technical approach

Data collection

Extension of web crawling toward social media, plus multimodal tracking via similarity-preserving hashes and chunking.

Content-based analysis

AI-generated text detection, stylometry, fact-checking agents, and scalable filtering.

Provenance-based analysis

Temporal diffusion analysis, actor identification, and network tracing of coordinated campaigns.

University of Passau contribution

The University of Passau coordinates SOURCE and focuses particularly on the bridge from OpenWebSearch.eu to disinformation tracing:

  • extension of existing OpenWebSearch data acquisition and retrieval workflows
  • provenance-oriented analysis of temporal diffusion and actor networks
  • scalable similarity-preserving hashing for reused text and images
  • integration of these components into shared HPC and storage workflows

This work builds directly on the Open Web Index and on OURRS as an access layer for retrieval and experimentation. The planned extension is to move beyond web-only indexing by adding social-media traces and image hashing so that disinformation artefacts can be linked, followed over time, and analysed as campaigns instead of isolated posts or pages.

Consortium

  • University of Passau, Chair of Data Science (Prof. Dr. Michael Granitzer): coordination; source-based detection, i.e. identifying and tracking media content on the web and analysing the actors and networks involved
  • University of Kassel, Deep Semantic Learning (Prof. Dr. Martin Potthast): content-based detection, including AI-generated text, stylometric authorship analysis, and evaluation of the methods
  • Open Search Foundation: building, engaging and connecting the communities around the tools; science communication
  • Leibniz Supercomputing Centre (LRZ) (Dr. Megi Sharikadze): computing, cloud and storage capacity for the SOURCE database on a federated infrastructure (including LEXIS)
  • Alliance4Europe (Benjamin Zeeb): use-case development and expert studies; the Counter Disinformation Network (130+ analysts); the FRIKI threat-intelligence database; integration of SOURCE results into the DISARM framework

The SOURCE harness

On top of the search index and the SOURCE analytics engine, Passau and Kassel build the SOURCE harness, currently a research prototype: an AI research assistant with an LLM agent that an analyst runs on their own machine. It searches web and social-media posts through ourrs.eu, reads platforms read-only, keeps every page it reads as evidence, maps what it finds to DISARM techniques and countermeasures, and writes its findings into a research wiki the analyst reviews and edits. It is developed with Alliance4Europe around the DISARM framework and its successor DISRUPT, and its use is to be agreed with the analyst community.
