Applying for a thesis

Theses at the Chair of Data Science follow a four-step path: topic selection at the Oberseminar, a scoped entry task, a research exposé, and formal registration. Registration starts the official six-month clock for Master's theses, so we accept a thesis only once the exposé is solid enough to defend a plan against it.

The four steps

  1. Topic selection. Attend the Oberseminar topic presentation (twice a semester). Pick one topic or prepare a one-pager for an open theme.
  2. Entry task. Each defined topic comes with a 1–2 week scoped task — a small experiment, a literature sketch, or a proof-of-concept. It tests the fit between you and the topic.
  3. Research exposé. Write a structured 4–6 page document (motivation, state of the art, research questions, methodology, work plan, limitations) and present it at the end-of-semester Oberseminar.
  4. Registration. Once the exposé is accepted, register the thesis with the examination office. Registration is binding and starts the official working period.

Time limits after registration

  • Master's thesis: six months of working time from the date of registration, as defined by the examination regulations. Do not register until the exposé has been accepted.
  • Bachelor's thesis: the working time is shorter — check the current examination regulations for your programme at the link below.
  • Extensions are possible only under the formal conditions of the examination office (typically illness with a medical certificate). Plan for the nominal period, not for an extension.

For current working periods and the formal registration process, seethe University of Passau thesis page.

Meeting Prof. Granitzer

Prof. Granitzer holds a weekly Q&A slot for thesis and research questions onMonday 14:00–16:00. The slot requires prior registration through the chair's secretariat — just walking in is not possible.

  • Register the slot through the secretariat at least a few days in advance.
  • Bring one concrete question or a short one-pager. Unfocused check-ins get deferred.
  • For open-theme applications (OUR²S / YOARS), align on scope in the Q&A before submitting the one-pager.

What a good exposé looks like

  • Motivation: why this problem matters now.
  • State of the art: what exists and where it falls short — not a tutorial summary.
  • Research questions: at least three specific, answerable questions.
  • Methodology: datasets, metrics, tools, and evaluation plan.
  • Work plan: 3–5 milestones with realistic dates inside the working period.
  • Limitations: mandatory. Absence of limitations signals insufficient scoping.
